The research's measured scheduler rules give the design: one ready queue per priority and no timeslice, so the reader sits above the game thread; `sceKernelDelayThread(0)` does not yield [S]. Codec speed step 1 (identical texture re-uploads are not writes, `codec_speed.md`) stays the first speed change; nothing in the research changes it.

## Checked and not changed

- **Matrices on the CPU** (the GE multiplies projection x view x world in 16-bit truncated maths [S]): our converters already give the GE the VU's own matrix as world, an identity view and a projection that passes x and y through and copies z into w with factor 1, scaling only depth (`ge/xconv.c` xc_xform, `ge/gelist.c`). Products with 1 and 0 are exact, so screen positions get no extra GE rounding [C]; only depth takes one more truncation.
- **Merging sprites** (0.85 us a sprite command [S]): the GE is not the bottleneck. GE wait is 0 in all 295 codec windows on the PSP; the only GE-bound window is the viewfinder (vb 24,400: 11.8 ms) [C: gl4c run 3]. A codec frame with faces is 4,238 PRIMs and 21.7 k words [C: gl5 emulator], about 3-5 ms of GE command time by psprecomp's fitted model [I], against a 115-140 ms CPU step.
- **ME code calling memcpy** (gcc's loop-to-memcpy pass): v30me6b's ME objects call nothing outside the ME unit [C: psp-objdump -dr].
- **Presenter thread / async flip**: measured no gain in a recompiled game [S]; dropped.
- **A PPSSPP dev build on Ffm's PC**: master still rounds plain VFPU adds toward zero, so it splits at vb 500 like 1.20.4 without our patch [C, the other thread's run].
- **Later or Ffm's call**: GE skinning for the faces (7.2x for one character [S], not exact), interpolated 60 Hz display (Star Fox 64 PSP; visual), ME eDRAM register values (new register writes), the 2026 toolchain (no gain on ARK-4).

**2. Register traffic at calls: small; the register-locals rewrite is not worth it now [C model].** Counted on the x86 twin of gl4c / gl5's game code (the v31 headers; `tools/dev/x86_gi.sh`, `tools/dev/lean/girun.sh`, `tools/dev/lean/ctxcount.py`; hashes (ee, iop) equal `prof/hashes_x86_codec_h2.txt` at all 295 / 316 shared points), every executed context load and store weighted with callcost.py's PSP instruction model (`prof/ctx_deck_v31.txt`, `prof/ctx_codec_v31.txt`):

| Window (100 steps) | PSP instructions a step | at 1 instruction a cycle | share of the game code's PSP time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aft deck, vb 29,401-29,500 | 0.60 M | 1.8 ms | 2.4% of 74.4 ms (logic 43.25 + draw 31.14, gl4c run 3) |
| Otacon call 1, vb 31,501-31,600 | 0.24 M | 0.7 ms | 2.0% of 36.8 ms (8.01 + 28.77) |

On the aft deck by place: call sites 34% (stores before and reloads after 15,831 calls a step), returns 29%, function entries 25%, write-through moves 8%, other 4%. By speed_notes §591's four causes: (1) polls: 0 stores (gone since lean step 1b); (2) indirect and host calls: 20 k instructions a step at the sites (3%), plus 26 k at the division-by-zero checks, whose stores run only when a divisor is 0, so in practice never; (3) reloads after calls: at most 27 k R and 5.5 k F a step (GCC drops the dead ones); (4) returns: 172 k (29%; lean step 1c already trimmed the closed functions). These instructions are 14-20% of the generated code's size (ELF and w00a), so their share of instruction-cache misses may be larger than their 2.4% of instructions [I]; even so a perfect liveness could remove only part of them: about 1-2.5 ms an aft-deck step at most [I], 0.5-1.2% of its 205.6 ms. Free Riders' -13 to -18% came from moving guest registers out of memory into locals, which regcache.py already does. The step's time is in the renderer: GS walk 51.98 + picture 76.90 of 205.64 ms.
- Cheaper than a rewrite if calls matter later: inline the hot tiny callees at their call sites (`P165de0`, 9 guest instructions, 1,216 entries a step; `P165f60` / `P165f78`, 5 instructions, 762; `S_w00a_P3bec20`, 4 instructions, 453) [I: 0.2-0.4 ms a deck step].
