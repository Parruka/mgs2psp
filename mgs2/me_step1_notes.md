# The picture on the Media Engine: build order step 1, the split in the x86 twin

2026-11-03, task #147: `me_handoff_design.md` section 6 step 1. x86 runs only: no PSP build was made for it. [C] = measured or read in our code and x86 runs; [I] = my inference.

## The answer

- **The split works on x86 and is exact.** The twin takes each transfer's copy at its deadline (the hook that ends the transfer's step), with the guard log laid over RAM, as the ME would. From vb 60 every frame is built two steps late from that copy; the boot's frames come from the bytes as they came, as the main CPU will draw them. Every state hash and every frame equals today's run: deck 252 hash lines and 11,766 frames, indoor route 378 and 17,664, codec route 400 and 18,931 (48,361 frames, 0 different) [C].
- **The guards and the log give back every byte.** From vb 60 on, the deadline copy of every transfer (153,177 / 223,262 / 264,047 on the deck / indoor / codec routes) equals its copy taken as it came, byte for byte. The only differences are in the boot, which the main CPU draws: 16 transfers at vb 1 and one walk at vb 34, 22 bytes [C].
- **A small arena works too.** With the arena forced to 2 MB (`MGS2_XLIVE_DLA=2048`), a frame whose copy is larger is drawn by the main CPU at its deadline with today's code, one frame at a time, the rest late from the copy: 1,303 / 1,238 / 1,238 frames (16,845 / 16,065 / 16,065 transfers) went to the main CPU, the counts of design 4.9, and every state hash and every frame again equals today's [C: `xd/out_a{d,i,c}.log`].
- **The take-back works at a frame's end with the picture's state kept, not cold.** Started cold, as design 4.6 had it, the picture differed from today's at all six points tried, mostly to the route's end. With the translator's state, the GS map, the live frame's depth window and the texture cache carried through, every frame equals today's at all six points [C] (section 5).
- **Four changes to the design** (made in `me_handoff_design.md` 4.1, 4.3, 4.5 and 4.6):
  1. **The ME's walk runs the Stage B lanes.** Without them the codec faces lost C1 from the first call at vb 15,759: 2,070 / 3,716 / 4,265 frames differed, every state hash equal (section 2). The lanes' code moves from the runtime's walk into the picture code, and each transfer's record carries what decides them.
  2. **The log is kept per transfer, not per frame.** An entry is a 64-byte line and the number of the step's transfers started before the store; transfer t gets the entries made after it started, so that it reads the bytes as they were when it started. A step has up to 19 transfers [C].
  3. **The guards log only lines that the step's transfers copy.** The runtime itself writes 3.2-4.5 GB into game RAM over a route while frames are in flight, almost all of it outside the copy. With the filter the log holds 17-19 lines a step on average and at most 490 (31 KB) after the boot [C]. Without it (the deck, `xd/out_gu.log`) the picture is still exact, but the log holds 4,328 lines a step on average (277 KB) and up to 17,699 (1.1 MB), and the ME would lay 666 million lines over the route instead of 2.7 million [C].
  4. **The take-back keeps the picture's state.** The translator's state, the GS map, the live frame's depth window and the texture cache last from frame to frame. A planned take-back comes at a frame's end and the main CPU carries on with that state in main RAM; only a fault on the ME makes it start cold (section 5).
- **For you:** nothing. Step 2 (the picture code made ME-safe, still on x86) is next.

## 1. What the twin does

The twin (`/home/claude/me13`, container only; files in `tools/dev/me13/`) is sb14's x86 harness (the gl16 code: trims on, C1 on, C2 off) with:

- **me12's writer tracker:** the game code built with every store reporting itself (`GEN_DG_TRACE`), and the runtime reporting its own writes into game RAM (the scratchpad DMA, the IOP-to-EE copies, the disc reads; `ME_GUARD_RT` in `runtime/ee_io.h`, `sif.h`, `iop_host.h`).
- **me11 b's copy set** (`MGS2_XLIVE_SNAPP`, `MGS2_XLIVE_SNAPR=12c..142,1ed`): the packets, the data they point to and the texture sources in the two ranges; the other texture sources are read from RAM at the late walk.
- **The deadline copy** (`MGS2_XLIVE_DL=60`): at the hook that ends a step, each of the step's transfers has its chain walked again and its spans copied from RAM with the guard log laid over it, as the ME would at its deadline. From vb 60 the late walk reads that copy; before vb 60 it reads the copy taken as the transfer came (the boot, which the main CPU draws). Each deadline copy is compared with the copy taken as the transfer came, range by range and byte by byte: a byte that differs is a write no guard logged.
- **The guards:** every store of the 12 G0 functions (found by the store's return address in their code ranges, `xd/g0.txt` from `nm -S`) and every write of the runtime into game RAM, from the step's first transfer to its deadline.
- **The late walk with the lanes** (me13 b, section 2) and **each transfer's own switches at the late walk** (me13 c, section 4).

## 2. The lanes in the ME's walk

- **What went wrong first** (me13 a, `xd/out_f{d,i,c}.log`): every state hash equal, but from vb 15,759 on 2,070 of the deck's 11,766 frames differed (indoor route to vb 31,000: 3,716 of 14,432; codec route to vb 31,800: 4,265 of 14,831), each with a different GE list [C]. At vb 15,759 the codec faces were drawn the CPU-lit way (vertex type 0x19e, 20-byte vertices, one pass) instead of C1's (0x342, 18 bytes, lit by the GE) [C: GE draw diff of that frame].
- **Why:** the Stage B lanes live in the runtime's walk (`runtime/ee_io.h`, `vif1_chain_fast`) and run only when that walk feeds the translator as it goes (the one walk). They hand the converters whole groups with a record; C1 needs the record of lane 4 (`ge/xconv.c` 711), C2 that of lane 2 and step 3's trims that of lane 1. The late walk (`ge/xlive.c`) had no lanes, so no records: the trims and C2 change nothing in the picture, but C1, on by default since gl15, does [C].
- **The fix** (me13 b): the late walk is `ge/xlive.c`'s walk with `vif1_chain_fast`'s lane block, the lanes' recognizers generated from `runtime/ee_io.h` (`tools/mk_lw.py` to `boot/lw_lanes.h`) and reading the copy. It runs only for the transfers the runtime's walk would have fed to the translator (VIF1 on the fast path), with that transfer's lanes and switches. The translator itself turns a lane down when it is mid-command, where the runtime's walk tested its own VIF state. A group a lane takes passes its tags and data to the packet hash as before (0 misses on all routes). On the deck the lanes took 5,775,578 groups in lane 1, 952,002 in lane 2, 61,958 in lane 3 and 9,780 in lane 4 [C].
- **With it** (`xd/out_l16.log`, then `out_g{d,i,c}.log`) every frame equals today's [C].
- **For the PSP split:** the lanes' code moves from the runtime's walk into the picture code's walk; the runtime's walk keeps what the game's state needs. Whether it keeps the lanes' quick skip of a group's tags is a timing question for step 4 [I].

- **Every G0 function's guard was used** on its routes, from 2 stores in flight (`S_w00a_P11e0a8`) to 1,608,532 (`P16d1ac`, the codec's text page) [C].
- **The log's key:** an entry is (line, the number of the step's transfers started before the store). The ME lays over transfer t's copy the entries whose number is t or more, the newest first, so the oldest wins: the bytes as they were when t started. The design's per-frame table (one entry a line a frame) would give a transfer that started after a logged store the bytes from before that store, which that transfer never read [I]. A line is logged at most once per number (`changed by the deadline` counts the entries whose bytes the deadline copy would otherwise have read new).
- **The filter:** the guards log a line only if one of the step's transfers started so far copies it (a bitmap of 64-byte lines, cleared at the deadline). The runtime's writes in flight are the scratchpad DMA, the IOP's copies and the disc reads, 108-128 KB a vblank, nearly all outside the copy [C]. Unfiltered on the deck the log took 50,797,584 lines, 50,393,610 of them the runtime's and 403,974 the G0 functions' (192,605 filtered); a step held 4,328 on average and 17,699 at most (vb 15,600-15,699), and every frame was still equal [C: `xd/out_gu.log`]. So the G0 guards could log without the filter at about twice the lines; the runtime's writes cannot [C for the counts]. On the PSP the bitmap needs the copy's spans, which only the ME walks (design 4.1): either the main CPU marks the lines in its own walk (about 0.6 ms a frame [I]), or the ME publishes the bitmap as it walks with a count of finished walks, and a guard logs every line of a transfer not yet walked [I]. Step 4's timing decides.
- **Writers the twin does not guard:** the kernel's ReferThreadStatus and ReferSemaStatus writes (`kernel.h`) and boot.c's syscalls 0x4b and 0x6f (the boot only); no byte came from them [C: every deadline copy equal from vb 60]. The game's unaligned 8-byte stores go through the shared helper `GSDU`, which the tracker files under its own name: it wrote in flight only in the next step (design G1t), never in a G0 function's own step on these routes [C]; on the PSP the G0 functions call a guarded copy of it [I].
- **Before the hand-over** the log reached 4,096 lines a step (the runtime's IOP copies at vb 0); the boot stays on the main CPU (design 4.10) [C].

## 4. Switches per transfer

The PSP builds flip g_opt and g_opt2 in turns every 100 vblanks. The late walk ran with the switches of the moment it ran, so a transfer made just before a turn would be drawn with the next turn's switches. me13 c takes the translator's switches (the lanes, the eager unpack and the mark, the texture memo and its front, the palette loads by identity) from the g_opt and g_opt2 each transfer's record carries, and the frame's start record carries the frame's own. On x86 the switches do not move, so the routes test the plumbing, not a turn [C]. For the design (4.1): each record carries the transfer's registers, whether the runtime's walk would feed it to the translator, its lanes, and g_opt and g_opt2.

- **What lasts from frame to frame** [C]: the game sets GS registers and loads textures and palettes into the GS's memory in one frame and draws with them in many later ones, and the picture code keeps its model of both: the translator's state (the GS registers, the GIF and VIF state, VU1's memory and the sources of its micro program, the GS front end: 29,528 bytes; `ge_e/xlate.c` `xl_keep_save`) and the GS map (what the GS's memory holds: 328,200 bytes of fixed tables, and its records). The live frame carries one more thing: its depth window (the range its depth map covers when a frame has none of its own, and the effects' class 3 window, the last frame's range: four floats, `ge/gelive.c` `gl_end`). The texture cache is part of it too, in one case found: the cache keeps a texture's texels as first converted for as long as its identity holds, and the map reads an upload's texels from EE RAM when a texture is converted, not when they were sent (`ge/gsup.c` `xf_bytes`; the open point of `renderer_notes.md` 11.10), so where those bytes change under the same identity only the cache still holds the texels as first drawn.
- **Cold, the picture is wrong until the game sends that state again.** At 1,500 (the opening) keeping the map alone leaves only the first frame different (it lacks the blend registers set before X: the translator's state), and keeping the translator alone leaves 172 (the opening's text page lacks glyphs uploaded before X: the map). On the deck from 6,000 every frame is right until vb 23,269, then 890 differ, which the map alone puts right; in the first codec call (15,800) 2,939 of 4,477, 2,049 of them from the next frame on, which the translator's state alone puts right, and the same 890 from vb 23,269; indoors and on the codec route every frame to the route's end.
- **Warm:** with the translator's state and the map kept (TBK 3) the frames from 1,500, 6,000 and 15,800 equal today's; at 24,000 and 33,000 the first frame differed alone (vb 24,001 and 33,001): more effect triangles drawn as the PS2's polygon (aux 29,856 and 208 bytes against 29,312 and 192), because the new live frame had no depth window yet. With the depth window kept as well (TBK 7, me13 f) every frame equals today's at five points. On the codec route from 36,000, 578 of 2,000 frames still differed, in 16 runs (36,001-36,015, 36,343-36,351, 36,487-36,501, ..., 38,529-39,065, 39,277-39,281, 39,509-39,999), each with today's GE list but other texels: the codec's subtitle text page alone (CLUT4, the GE's 512 x 128 for the game's 512 x 120 image, drawn by one sprite to PSP screen rows 153-221 that reads texel rows 0 to 120): 2,024 of its 32,768 bytes, all in rows 120-127 below the image, 253 bytes a row, with 132 nonzero texels a row in today's run and all 512 after the take-back, the same in the three frames saved (vb 36,001, 36,343 and 36,487; `gerruns.sh`, `gercmp.py`) [C]. The sprite's last screen row reads row 120: the faint line of #84 (`renderer_notes.md` 11.18), which the PS2's own frames do not show. Today's run drew the page as it had converted it before 36,000; converted again after the take-back, rows 120-127 came out otherwise under the same identity [C], as when an upload's source bytes change after it was sent [I]. With the texture cache kept too (TBK 15, me13 g) every frame equals today's at all six points; what still starts afresh at the take-back is the rest of the live frame and the GE list.
- **"Shown frames only"** (design 4.6 cited it for the cold start) left frames out but kept all of this state, so it showed that a frame needs no earlier frame's draws, not that it needs none of their GS state [C: these runs].
- **For the PSP** (design 4.6, rewritten): a planned take-back comes at a frame's end: the main CPU stops queueing transfers, the ME finishes the frames already queued, writes back its cache over the picture's state in main RAM (the translator's state, the map, the depth window and the texture cache) and rests, and the main CPU carries on with that state, as at the boot's hand-over (4.10) the other way round. The frames too large for a 2 MB arena (4.9) and a test build's turns go this way. An unplanned stop (an exception or a hang on the ME: a fault of ours) may leave the state half written, so the main CPU starts cold: safe, but the picture is wrong as in the cold column until the game sends the state again. A copy of the state at each frame's end would cover that too, but it would have to hold the texture cache's texels as well (up to its budget, 2,688 KB in these runs) and be made every frame: not planned; a fault is found and fixed on x86 and the emulator first. The PSP does not sleep in a run: the harness holds the power lock [C: `me_startup_study.md`].

## 6. What the twin does that the PSP build does otherwise

- The texture sources left out of the copy are chosen by a bitmap of the 16-byte pieces that were upload sources when the transfer came, which the twin's GS map keeps; on the PSP the runtime's walk publishes them per transfer (design 4.1) [C for the twin].
- The deadline copy is the main thread's second walk at the step's end; the ME's speed is not modelled. The small-arena runs model the 2 MB eDRAM case only (design 4.9).
